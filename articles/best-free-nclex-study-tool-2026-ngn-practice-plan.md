## 2. NCSBN Candidate Resources — Best Official Free Resource

NCSBN resources should be part of every NCLEX plan because they come from the organization behind the exam. They clarify client needs categories, exam structure, and the clinical judgment model. This helps you avoid studying only the topics that feel familiar.

The limitation is that NCSBN resources are not a complete daily practice system. They tell you what the exam measures, but they do not replace a question bank, rationales, or readiness exams. Use them at the beginning of your plan and again before test day to make sure your practice routine matches the exam blueprint.

## 3. RegisteredNurseRN — Best Free Content Explainer

RegisteredNurseRN is a useful free resource when you miss questions because a content area is weak. The videos and notes can help with med-surg, maternity, pediatrics, fundamentals, pharmacology, labs, and common disease processes.

The key is to use content review after practice exposes a gap. If you watch videos for hours without answering questions, it can feel productive while doing little for test readiness. A better routine is: miss a question, identify the weak concept, watch or read a short explanation, then answer more questions on that concept.

## 4. Nurselabs — Best Free Supplemental Quiz Library

Nurselabs offers free NCLEX-style quizzes and review material across many nursing categories. It is helpful when you need extra repetitions in a weak topic, especially if your school resources have expired.

Because topic quizzes are usually organized by subject, use them for remediation rather than final readiness. The real NCLEX mixes subjects and asks you to prioritize without telling you the category. After using Nurselabs to repair a weak area, return to mixed NGN practice so you can apply the concept in context.

## 5. SimpleNursing Free Content — Best Visual Review

SimpleNursing's free videos and clips are helpful for visual learners. Mnemonics can make medications, endocrine concepts, electrolytes, and disease processes easier to remember.

The caution is that memory tricks are not enough. After a video, write one NCLEX application sentence: "If I see this cue, the safest nursing action is..." Then do practice questions. The goal is not to remember a phrase; it is to choose the safest action under exam conditions.

## 6. Nurse Sarah and Other Educator Channels — Best Fundamentals Support

Nurse educator channels can give calm, clear explanations when fundamentals feel shaky. They are especially useful for lab interpretation, dosage basics, infection control, respiratory concepts, cardiac topics, and maternal-child review.

Avoid playlist overload. Pick one weak area, review it, and then move back to active practice. Passing comes from repeated decisions, not from completing every free video available online.

## 7. Quizlet and Anki — Best Free Recall Tools

Flashcards are excellent for facts: antidotes, lab values, isolation precautions, developmental milestones, electrolyte symptoms, and medication classes. They are not enough for clinical judgment because they remove patient context.

The best flashcards come from your missed questions. If you missed digoxin toxicity, make a card about symptoms and nursing action. If you confused airborne and droplet precautions, make a comparison card. Keep flashcards short and review them daily.

## 8. School ATI, HESI, Kaplan, or Elsevier Access — Best Already-Paid Resource

Many graduates still have access to school resources. ATI, HESI, Kaplan, Elsevier, faculty remediation packets, predictor reports, or library databases may already be included in tuition.

Before buying anything new, check your student portal and email your program. If the tool includes rationales and performance reports, use it. If it lacks NGN case volume or readiness feedback, add only that missing layer rather than replacing everything.

## 9. UWorld Free Samples — Best Premium Preview

UWorld is a respected paid question bank with detailed rationales. Its free sample questions can help you decide whether the style fits your learning needs.

For budget-conscious students, the issue is cost. If the price is stressful, compare lower-cost options before subscribing. A tool only helps if you can use it consistently without feeling pressured to rush through it because the subscription is expensive.

## 10. Archer Free Samples — Best Budget-Paid Preview

Archer is often considered by students looking for a lower-cost traditional review option. Free samples can show whether the interface, rationales, and question style feel useful.

Do not rely on samples as a readiness plan. A few preview questions cannot show whether you are improving across management of care, pharmacology, maternity, pediatrics, mental health, and NGN case studies. Use samples to compare style, then choose one primary practice system.
