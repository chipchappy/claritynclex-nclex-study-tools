## Top 10 Free NCLEX Study Tools

Free does not automatically mean low quality. The problem is that free resources are fragmented. One tool may explain labs well, another may have topic quizzes, and another may clarify NGN item types. A strong free plan connects those resources into a weekly routine: mixed practice, rationale review, content repair, and rest.

Before adding paid prep, audit your free options. If you already have school access, official NCLEX information, YouTube content, and a structured error log, you may only need one small upgrade for realistic question volume. If you do not have enough NGN-style mixed practice, that gap matters because the real exam is not organized by textbook chapter.

## 2. NCSBN NCLEX Resources — Best Official Free Starting Point

NCSBN resources are the first stop because they come from the organization behind the NCLEX. They help you understand the exam plan, client needs categories, Next Gen item types, and the clinical judgment model.

These resources will not replace daily practice, but they prevent wasted studying. If you know the exam is measuring clinical judgment, you can study differently: look for cues, compare priorities, decide on safe actions, and evaluate outcomes. Review NCSBN materials early, then revisit them when you begin practicing case studies.

## 3. RegisteredNurseRN — Best Free Content Refreshers

RegisteredNurseRN is useful when you need to repair a specific knowledge gap. Topics like lab values, endocrine disorders, cardiac conditions, respiratory care, maternity, pediatrics, and pharmacology can feel easier after a clear review.

The key is timing. Do not turn free videos into your whole study plan. Watch them after your missed questions reveal a pattern. For example, if you keep missing heart failure priority questions, review that topic and then immediately answer more practice questions. Content review should serve practice, not replace it.

## 4. Nurselabs — Best Free Topic Quiz Supplement

Nurselabs can help with topic-based quizzes, quick rationales, and review lists. It is especially useful when you want extra exposure to fundamentals, medical-surgical nursing, maternity, psychiatric nursing, and pharmacology.

Because topic quizzes are organized by category, they are best for remediation rather than final readiness. The NCLEX mixes topics and asks you to prioritize. Use Nurselabs to strengthen weak areas, then return to mixed practice so you do not become dependent on knowing the topic category before reading the question.

## 5. SimpleNursing Free Content — Best Visual Review

SimpleNursing’s free videos and social clips are helpful for visual learners. Mnemonics, diagrams, and short explanations can make difficult content easier to remember.

Use visual review for high-yield concepts, but check whether you can apply the concept in a question. A memory trick is only useful if it helps you choose the safest nursing action. After watching, write one sentence in your notes: “On the NCLEX, this means I should…” Then do practice questions to test the idea.

## 6. Nurse Sarah and Other Educator Channels — Best Fundamentals Support

Nurse educator channels can be excellent for fundamentals, dosage concepts, disease reviews, and nursing interventions. They are also accessible when you need a calm explanation after a stressful study session.

The risk is playlist overload. Choose one weak topic per day and stop when the knowledge gap is repaired. Then move back into active recall, questions, and rationales. Passing usually comes from repeated decision practice, not from completing every video on the internet.

## 7. School ATI, HESI, Kaplan, Elsevier, or Faculty Resources — Best Already-Paid Tools

Many students have access to tools they forgot about. ATI modules, HESI review, Kaplan resources, Elsevier questions, textbooks, library databases, faculty remediation, or school predictor reports may already be included in tuition.

Before buying anything new, check your student portal and email your program if needed. If these tools include rationales, topic reports, or practice tests, use them. If they are weak on NGN cases or mixed practice, then add only the missing layer instead of starting over with a completely new expensive product.

## 8. Public Library and Open Educational Resources — Best Free Books and Databases

Public libraries sometimes provide test-prep books, e-books, database access, study rooms, and free printing. Nursing students often overlook this because digital question banks dominate the conversation.

Books are best for structured content review and quick refreshers. They are less ideal for simulating the NCLEX, especially NGN case formats. Use library resources for content outlines, then pair them with active questions and a planner.

## 9. Free NCLEX Study Planner — Best Scheduling Tool

A free planner can prevent random studying. Plan your week around categories: mixed question sets, rationale review, weak-topic repair, NGN case practice, rest, and a short weekly review.

A realistic planner matters more than an intense one. If you work full time or have family responsibilities, a four-hour daily plan may collapse by day three. A better free plan might be 45 minutes of questions, 30 minutes of rationales, and 15 minutes updating your error log. Consistency beats a dramatic schedule you cannot maintain.

## 10. Error Log — Best Free Remediation System

The best free NCLEX tool is an error log. It costs nothing and makes every missed question more valuable.

Track the topic, item type, missed cue, why the correct answer is safest, why your answer was tempting, and the next action. Good entries are specific. Instead of “review maternity,” write “missed late decelerations; review fetal monitoring; do 10 priority questions tomorrow.” This turns frustration into a clear plan.
